AI History in Papers

The history of AI research from 1943 to today, followed through its original papers

Key point

  • Every dot on the timeline is an original paper, with a link to the source, one sentence quoted from it with a page number, and one or two lines on what changed.
  • Lineage lines are solid only where a citation is confirmed. Influences stated in textbooks without a confirmed citation are dotted.
  • The selection rules were registered before the list was made (see Method and limits). Priority disputes show both sides' bibliographies and make no ruling.

What this site does

The history of AI research has been written in many places: the Wikipedia timelines, the Computer History Museum's timeline, the history chapters of textbooks, one researcher's long annotated history. This site does not replace them. It adds one thing: every point is tied to an original paper. Dates are first-public dates rather than popularly quoted years, "what changed" is written from the paper's own sentences, and lines of influence distinguish confirmed citations from unconfirmed attributions.

Numbers only support the narrative. Citation counts appear small on each card with the date they were read, and there is no ranking. The page on winters and booms re-examines the two conventional winters (around 1974 and around 1987) against paper-title counts, conference attendance and public funding records.
