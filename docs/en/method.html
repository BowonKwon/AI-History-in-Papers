Method and limits

The selection rules were registered before the list was made; dates come from the originals and lineage lines from citation data. This page records the procedure and the limits it carries.

Selecting papers — registered 2026-11-03

Six reference works were fixed first: Nilsson, The Quest for Artificial Intelligence (2010); Russell & Norvig, Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach, 4th ed. (2020); Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville, Deep Learning (2016); Sutton & Barto, Reinforcement Learning, 2nd ed. (2018); Prince, Understanding Deep Learning (2023); Jurafsky & Martin, Speech and Language Processing, 3rd ed. draft. Version 1.1 added a seventh, Murphy, Probabilistic Machine Learning: An Introduction (2022). Four inclusion rules apply.

  • R1 Textbooks. At least two of the reference works cite the original publication. (150 papers)
  • R2 Lineage. At least three already-selected papers cite it (OpenAlex or the citing paper's reference list). (33)
  • R3 Awards. The Turing Award or Nobel Prize citation names the achievement. (15)
  • R4 Recent (2021 onward). Peer-reviewed publication or public review record, original text checked. (28)

The result is 214 papers. Textbook matching compared bibliography text by title, author and year automatically, with samples checked by hand; hits corrected by hand are listed with the evidence in textbook_overrides.csv. Nilsson's book keeps its references in chapter endnotes, so the whole text (the author's public PDF, Internet Archive capture of 2026-10-11) was used as the comparison text; a work named by title or by author and year in the body counts as cited. Schmidhuber's "Annotated History of Modern AI and Deep Learning" (2022) is not a reference work here; it is used only as one side in the priority boxes.

Revision 1.1.2 — 2026-11-11

The cards of seven books that had been empty for lack of the original are now filled. Bellman (1957 edition, sixth printing), Minsky–Papert (1988 expanded edition), Holland (1992 MIT Press edition), Marr (2010 MIT Press reissue) and Pearl (1988) were read in the book; Schank–Abelson (1977) and Quinlan's C4.5 (1993) were read only in a publisher's preview and are marked "partially checked". Each card names the edition and page read. No card is "not accessible" any longer. Seventeen citation links read from the books' reference lists were added. Four papers whose cards were filled in 1.0.1 but whose chapter text still said "not read" (McCarthy 1960, Buchanan–Feigenbaum 1978, Werbos 1982, Boser et al. 1992) now agree with their cards. Selection is unchanged.

Revision 1.1.1 — 2026-11-08

Korean edition only: technical terms rendered in Korean now carry the English original in parentheses at their first appearance on each page. The term list is glossary_ko.csv. Selection and cards are unchanged.

Revision 1.1 — 2026-11-07

Two changes. First, statistical machine learning lacked a textbook, so Murphy, Probabilistic Machine Learning: An Introduction (MIT Press 2022), was added as the seventh reference work; its bibliography was taken from the author's public draft PDF (version of 2025-04-18). Second, the candidate pool was widened: papers cited in OpenAlex by at least three selected papers and listed by at least three reference works were added when they were not yet candidates. Textbooks, surveys, and other versions of a contribution already on the timeline were left out. Added papers were selected only under the four rules above. The 16 works added, with their citation counts and reference works, are listed in pool-1.1-added.csv. One of them, Jelinek 1976, was later found to have been matched in one reference work (Jurafsky & Martin) to a different paper by the same author. Corrected, it is matched in two reference works, short of the three the widening requires, so under the registered condition it was taken out of the pool and is not selected. Fifteen remain in the pool.

Dates

A point sits at the first public date: the earliest documented technical report, thesis, arXiv v1 or conference presentation. Journal issue dates, conference dates and reprint years are listed on the card. OpenAlex publication years were not used because they mix in reprint and conference dates (23 of the 234 candidates for 1.0 differed).

Checking the originals

Each card's quotation and page were read from the stated version (journal, conference, or arXiv version number). By status: 202 verified, 12 partially checked, 0 not accessible. "Not accessible" means no legitimate public copy was found; those cards carry no quotation. A few papers with no public copy (for example Werbos's 1974 thesis and his 1982 proceedings chapter) were read from copies of the printed or original text obtained by the editor, as the card's "version read" says.

Lineage lines

Three kinds: in OpenAlex's reference list (548), not in OpenAlex but seen in the citing paper's own reference list (738), and stated in a textbook without a confirmed citation (0, dotted). OpenAlex alone leaves large gaps: among the candidates, 30% of pre-1990 records and 40% of arXiv records had zero references.

Priority disputes

Where "who was first" is disputed (backpropagation, convolutional networks, deep network training, residual connections), the boxes place both sides' original bibliographies and sentences side by side and make no ruling. Secondary sources that do rule are introduced as claims, with their source.

Bias

The reference set is English-language textbooks, and the resulting bias is not hidden. Early neural-network work from the Soviet Union and Japan (Ivakhnenko, Amari, Fukushima) and Linnainmaa in Finland sometimes fails the two-textbook rule; such papers appear in the priority boxes. The country distribution of author affiliations can be computed from this version's papers.csv and the OpenAlex records.

Sources and terms
